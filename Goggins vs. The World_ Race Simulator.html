How the model works

Each runner is defined by a flat marathon time (editable above). Finish time follows a Riegel-style curve: time ∝ distance1.06 up to 26.2 miles, then distancek beyond it (k is the fatigue slider; elite 100-mile marks sit near 1.25). The result is multiplied by a terrain factor (my rough guess per course: heat, climbing, altitude, night, trails), then rest time is added once moving time passes 20 hours. Goggins’ lane uses his actual finish time, with the same fatigue curve shaping his pacing. Kipchoge has never raced an ultra, so his lane is an extrapolation, not a prediction. The “normal person” lane assumes a walk/jog finish; most untrained people would not complete these distances or would miss official cutoffs. Marathon times are assumptions: Kipchoge 2:01:09 (his Berlin 2022 mark), pro 2:10, healthy 4:30, normal 6:30. Goggins results come from ultrarunning.com, DUV statistics and his official site; DNFs are excluded. Sim output is for fun, not a scientific forecast.
